## 1. Linguistic Overview & Sociolinguistic Context

Dagbani is the primary language of the Dagbamba (*Dagbana* singular) people, numbering over 3.2 million speakers across the Northern Region of Ghana and contiguous areas of northern Togo. The language serves as the regional lingua franca and administrative center in Tamale (the commercial and political capital of the Northern Region) and Yendi (the historical and spiritual seat of the *Yaa Naa*, the King of Dagbon).

### 1.1 Dialectal Stratification
The language is divided into two primary regional dialects and a closely related southern sister variety:
1. **Tòmòsílí (Western Dialect)**: Centered around Tamale, Savelugu, Kumbungu, and Tolon. Characterized by high consonant lenition, vowel coalescence ($/bg/ \rightarrow [\upsilon]$, $/gs/ \rightarrow [x]$), and progressive [+ATR] vowel raising. It serves as the primary standard for broadcast media, primary education curricula, and published literature.
2. **Nàyàhílí / Kàtìndù (Eastern Dialect)**: Centered in Yendi, Mion, and Gushegu. Characterized by conservative phonetic preservation of velar plosives, distinct noun class root vowels, and retracted tongue root ([-ATR]) stability in specific noun bases.
3. **Nànùndí**: Spoken by the Nanumba people in Bimbila and surrounding districts. Mutually intelligible with Dagbani, sharing over 90% lexical and phonological congruence.

### 1.2 Sister Languages & Genetic Neighbors
Dagbani belongs to the Southeast Mabia cluster of Western Oti-Volta languages. It shares high structural and lexical mutual intelligibility with:
- **Mampruli** (`maw`): ~95% structural congruence; spoken immediately to the north.
- **Gurene / Frafra** (`gur`): Upper East Region of Ghana.
- **Kusaal** (`kus`): Bawku municipal area.
- **Dagaare / Waali** (`dga`/`wlx`): Upper West Region of Ghana.
- **Mooré** (`mos`): Spoken across central Burkina Faso.

## 3. Vocalic System & Advanced Tongue Root (ATR) Harmony

Dagbani possesses an asymmetric **11-vowel system** partitioned into **6 short vowels** (/i, e, ɨ, a, o, u/) and **5 long vowels** (/iː, eː, aː, oː, uː/), governed by an Advanced Tongue Root ([±ATR]) harmony mechanism.

### 3.3 Harmony Mechanics, Directionality, and Opacity

1. **Harmonic Partitions**:
   - **[+ATR] Harmonic Set**: $\mathcal{H}_{[+\text{ATR}]} = \{ /i/, /e/, /o/, /u/, [a̘] \}$
   - **[-ATR] Harmonic Set**: $\mathcal{H}_{[-\text{ATR}]} = \{ /ɨ/, /ɛ/, /ɔ/, [ʊ], /a/ \}$

2. **Suffix-to-Root Harmony (Regressive Spreading)**:
   - When a noun root containing a [-ATR] mid vowel attaches a [+ATR] suffix (`-o`, `-e`), the root vowel obligatorily harmonizes to [+ATR]:
     $$\text{Root } /\text{dɔr}/ \text{ (illness)} + \text{Suffix } /-\text{o}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[dóró]} \quad (\text{Singular})$$
     $$\text{Root } /\text{dɔr}/ \text{ (illness)} + \text{Suffix } /-\text{tɨ}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[dɔ́rtɨ́]} \quad (\text{Plural})$$
     $$\text{Root } /\text{kɔr}/ \text{ (appetite)} + \text{Suffix } /-\text{e}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[kore]} \quad (\text{Singular})$$
     $$\text{Root } /\text{kɔr}/ \text{ (appetite)} + \text{Suffix } /-\text{ɨsɨ}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[kɔ́rɨ́sɨ́]} \quad (\text{Plural})$$

3. **Root-to-Suffix Harmony (Progressive Spreading)**:
   - When a root containing a [+ATR] high vowel (`/i/`, `/u/`) attaches a suffix with a [-ATR] vowel, [+ATR] spreads rightward into the suffix:
     $$\text{Root } /\text{dir}/ \text{ (eat/food)} + \text{Suffix } /-\text{gʊ}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[dirigu]} \quad (\text{Singular utensil})$$
     $$\text{Root } /\text{dɨr}/ \text{ (eat)} + \text{Suffix } /-\text{tɨ}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[dɨ́rɨ́tɨ́]} \quad (\text{Plural})$$

4. **Height-Conditioned Agreement Constraint**:
   - Harmony propagation requires height agreement between trigger and target: `[+high]` triggers `[+high]` raising; `[-high]` triggers `[-high]` raising. Cross-height spreading across morpheme boundaries is blocked unless mediated by glide transitions.

5. **Opaque Consonant Blockers $\{/l/, /s/, /r/\}$**:
   - The coronal alveolar consonants **`/l/`, `/s/`, and `/r/`** act as opaque barriers that strictly terminate the progressive spread of [+ATR] from root `/i/`:
     $$\text{Root } /\text{pil}/ \text{ (start/cover)} + \text{Suffix } /-\text{i}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[pílɨ́]} \quad (\text{not } *[pili])$$
     $$\text{Root } /\text{jir}/ \text{ (startle)} + \text{Suffix } /-\text{gi}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[jírɨ́gɨ́]} \quad (\text{not } *[jirigi])$$
     $$\text{Root } /\text{jin}/ \text{ (house)} + \text{Suffix } /-\text{si}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[jínsɨ́]} \quad (\text{not } *[jinsi])$$

6. **Domain Barrier Principle**:
   - Vowel harmony operates exclusively within the **lexical phonological word** (stem + affixes). Harmony **never** crosses compound borders or syntactic phrase boundaries:
     $$\left[\text{bi}\right]_{\text{root}} + \left[\text{bɛ́'ʊ́}\right]_{\text{suf}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{[bí\ bɛ́ʔʊ́]} \quad (\text{'ugly child', strictly } *\text{bí béʔú})$$

## 4. Tone System and Suprasegmental Prosody

### 4.1 Register Tones and Tone-Bearing Units (TBUs)
Dagbani is a **register tone language** characterized by **two primary level tones** and **systematic downstep**:
1. **High Tone (H / ´)**: Higher pitch register ($F_0$ peak).
2. **Low Tone (L / `)**: Lower pitch register ($F_0$ baseline).
3. **Downstep (!H)**: Lowering of the pitch ceiling of a High tone when preceded by an overt or floating Low tone ($H \rightarrow !H$).
4. **Tone-Bearing Unit (TBU)**:
   - Each vowel mora constitutes $1\text{ TBU}$ (Short $V = 1\text{ TBU}$, Long $VV = 2\text{ TBUs}$, Diphthong $V_1V_2 = 2\text{ TBUs}$).
   - **Moraic Nasal TBUs**: Syllable-final nasals (`/m, n, ŋ/`) in coda position function as **independent, tone-bearing moras**:
     - *sɔ́ŋ* `[sɔ́ŋ́]` ('mat', High tone coda nasal, bimoraic).
     - *ǹ-dá* `[ǹ-dá]` ('I bought', Low tone syllabic nasal prefix).

### 4.2 Tone Terracing (Automatic Downdrift)
In multi-word utterances, alternating High and Low tones generate a terraced pitch staircase:
$$\text{In a sequence } H_1 - L_1 - H_2 - L_2 - H_3\dots, \quad \text{Pitch}(H_2) < \text{Pitch}(H_1), \quad \text{Pitch}(H_3) < \text{Pitch}(H_2)$$
This automatic pitch downdrift causes the absolute fundamental frequency ($F_0$) of phrase-final High tones to align acoustically near or below initial Low tones.

### 4.4 Grammatical Functions of Tone
- **Tense/Aspect/Mood (TAM) Marking**: Overriding verbal melodies signal aspect. Perfective verbs take a Low root melody with High suffix (*chàŋ-yá*), whereas Imperfectives take High root melodies (*chán-dí*).
- **Noun Class Pluralization**: Suffixation triggers tone shifts on nominal stems (*dá-á* [H-H] $\rightarrow$ *dá-hí* [H-H]; *dó-ó* [H-H] $\rightarrow$ *dɔ́r-tí* [H-H]).
- **Negation Polarity**: Negative particles (*kù* 'future neg', *bì* 'past neg', *dī* 'prohibitive') enforce tone polarity inversion on subsequent verb stems.

## 7. Grapheme-to-Phoneme (G2P) & Morphophonology

### 7.1 Deterministic G2P Transformation Pipeline
The conversion from written Dagbani text (BGL Unicode or ASCII fallback) to phonetic IPA representations follows an 8-stage pipeline:

```
[Raw Dagbani Text]
       │
       ▼  Stage 1: Unicode NFC Normalization & Punctuation Standardization
       ▼  Stage 2: ASCII Disambiguation (gh -> ɣ, zh -> ʒ, ngm -> ŋm, ng -> ŋ)
       ▼  Stage 3: Digraph Tokenization (kp, gb, ŋm, ny, ch, sh)
       ▼  Stage 4: Contextual Palatalization (k, g, s, z, ŋ -> tʃ, dʒ, ʃ, ʒ, ɲ before front V)
       ▼  Stage 5: Labial-Coronal Mutation (kp, gb, ŋm -> tp, db, nm before front V)
       ▼  Stage 6: Intervocalic Lenition & Debuccalization (d -> r, s -> h, g -> ɣ/ʔ)
       ▼  Stage 7: Epenthetic Vowel Insertion & Homorganic Nasal Assimilation
       ▼  Stage 8: Tonal Tagging & Prosodic Assignment
       │
       ▼
[Phonetic IPA Sequence]
```

### 7.2 Core Morphophonological Rules

1. **Homorganic Nasal Prefix Assimilation**:
   The 1SG pronominal prefix and nominalizing prefix `/N-/` assimilates to the place of articulation of the root-initial consonant:
   $$/N-/ + C \longrightarrow \begin{cases} [m] & \text{before } /p, b, f, v/ \quad (\text{e.g. } \text{/N-bɔ/ } \rightarrow \text{ [m̀-bɔ́]}) \\ [n] & \text{before } /t, d, s, z, l/ \quad (\text{e.g. } \text{/N-da/ } \rightarrow \text{ [ǹ-dá]}) \\ [\ɲ] & \text{before } /t͡ʃ, d͡ʒ, ʃ, ʒ, j/ \quad (\text{e.g. } \text{/N-chaŋ/ } \rightarrow \text{ [ɲ̀-t͡ʃáŋ]}) \\ [\ŋ] & \text{before } /k, ɡ, ɣ/ \quad (\text{e.g. } \text{/N-ka/ } \rightarrow \text{ [ŋ̀-ká]}) \\ [\ŋ͡m] & \text{before } /k͡p, ɡ͡b/ \quad (\text{e.g. } \text{/N-kpa/ } \rightarrow \text{ [ŋ͡m̀-k͡pá]})\end{cases}$$

2. **Intervocalic Lenition of `/d/` to `[r]`**:
   Alveolar plosive `/d/` surfaces as alveolar tap `[r]` intervocalically in native stems:
   $$/\text{kad-a}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[kárá]} \quad (\text{'chase away', pluractional})$$

3. **Intervocalic Debuccalization of `/s/` to `[h]`**:
   Voiceless alveolar fricative `/s/` debuccalizes to glottal `[h]` intervocalically:
   $$/\text{bii-si}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[bíhí]} \quad (\text{'children'})$$
   *Blocker*: Debuccalization is strictly blocked when `/s/` is preceded by a coda nasal:
   $$/\text{duun-si}/ \longrightarrow \mathbf{[dúúnsí]} \quad (\text{'rooms', strictly } *\text{duunhi})$$

4. **Hiatus Elision at Syntactic Boundaries**:
   When a vowel-final word precedes a vowel-initial word in rapid speech, $V_1$ elides:
   $$V_1 \# V_2 \longrightarrow \emptyset \# V_2 \quad (\text{e.g. } \text{'tí ó'} \rightarrow \text{'t\'ó'} \text{ [t-ó]})$$

## 8. Syntactic Blueprint & Metrical Tense System

### 8.1 Canonical SVO 6-Slot Clause Structure
Dagbani syntax adheres strictly to **Subject-Verb-Object (SVO)** order. Declarative clauses are structured across 6 linear slots:

$$\text{[Slot 1: Subject]} \longrightarrow \text{[Slot 2: Time-Depth Marker]} \longrightarrow \text{[Slot 3: Aspect/Mood]} \longrightarrow \text{[Slot 4: Verb Base]} \longrightarrow \text{[Slot 5: Focus Particle]} \longrightarrow \text{[Slot 6: Object/Adjunct]}$$

| Slot | Syntactic Category | Morpheme Examples | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Slot 1** | Subject (NP / Pronoun) | *ó* (3SG), *tí* (1PL), *nádòo* ('chief') | Agent or Experiencer |
| **Slot 2** | Time-Depth Marker (TDM) | *dí* (hodiernal), *sá* (hesternal), *dáá* (pre-hesternal) | Metrical remoteness anchor |
| **Slot 3** | Preverbal Aspect / Mood | *yɛ̀n* (future), *yáá* (habitual), *shírí* (affirmative) | Aspectual modifier |
| **Slot 4** | Verb Base | *chàŋ* ('go'), *dī* ('eat'), *nyɛ̄* ('see') | Lexical verbal nucleus |
| **Slot 5** | Discourse / Focus Particle | *mì* (declarative), *lá* (contrastive focus) | Pragmatic marker |
| **Slot 6** | Object / Adverbial | *shìkúrú* ('school'), *zúŋɔ́* ('today') | Patient / Temporal adjunct |

### 8.2 Three-Way Metrical Tense (Time-Depth) System

| Time-Depth Tier | Marker | Past Scope | Future Scope | Example Sentence | Gloss |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hodiernal** | **dí** / **díín** | Earlier today (hours ago) | Later today (near future) | *Ń dí chàŋ shìkúrú zúŋɔ́.* | 'I went to school earlier today.' |
| **Hesternal** | **sá** / **sán** | Yesterday (1 day ago) | Tomorrow (1 day ahead) | *Ń sá chàŋ shìkúrú.* | 'I went to school yesterday.' |
| **Pre-Hesternal** | **dáá** / **dáán** | 2+ days ago (remote past) | 2+ days ahead (remote future) | *Ń dáá chàŋ shìkúrú.* | 'I went to school days/years ago.' |
