**Document Version**: 1.0.0

## 1. Introduction & Historical Evolution

The modern Dagbani writing system was officially standardized in 1998 by the Bureau of Ghana Languages (BGL), following the recommendations of the 1996 Dagbani Orthography Committee. It replaced historical missionary and early colonial scripts, introducing a unified Latin alphabet with 5 special International African Institute (IAI) characters and standardized digraphs.

### 1.1 Key Historical Milestones
1. **Ajami Tradition (17th–20th Century)**: Arabic script adapted with diacritics for Dagbani labial-velars and vowels (documented by Afa Yusif Ajura, Tamale).
2. **Early Latin Standard (1941–1995)**: Blair & Tamakloe (1941) and GILLBT (1968) introduced Latin spelling, but lacked consistent representation for open-mid vowels (`ɛ, ɔ`) and velar fricatives (`ɣ`).
3. **1998 BGL Standard**: Established the current 28-character alphabet and digraph set, fully adopted in national education, media, and publishing.

## 3. Bidirectional ASCII <-> BGL Transliteration Rules

Because standard keyboard layouts often lack native keys for `ɛ, ɔ, ŋ, ɣ, ʒ`, digital communication (SMS, social media, older web texts) commonly relies on ASCII approximations.

### 3.1 Transliteration Mapping Engine

```
ASCII Input                                            Canonical BGL
====================================================================
"gh" / "Gh" / "GH"                      ───►           "ɣ" / "Ɣ"
"zh" / "Zh" / "ZH"                      ───►           "ʒ" / "Ʒ"
"ngm" / "Ngm" / "NGM"                   ───►           "ŋm" / "Ŋm" / "ŊM"
"ng" (coda or before vowel)             ───►           "ŋ" / "Ŋ"
"ny" / "Ny"                             ───►           "ny" / "Ny" (unchanged)
"kp" / "Kp"                             ───►           "kp" / "Kp" (unchanged)
"gb" / "Gb"                             ───►           "gb" / "Gb" (unchanged)
"sh" / "Sh"                             ───►           "sh" / "Sh" (unchanged)
"ch" / "Ch"                             ───►           "ch" / "Ch" (unchanged)
"e" (in -ATR lexical roots)              ───►           "ɛ" (contextual/lexicon)
"o" (in -ATR lexical roots)              ───►           "ɔ" (contextual/lexicon)
```

### 3.2 Digraph Parsing Disambiguation Rules
- **Rule 1: Priority Ordering**: Always match 3-letter clusters first (`ngm` $\rightarrow$ `ŋm`), then 2-letter digraphs (`gh` $\rightarrow$ `ɣ`, `zh` $\rightarrow$ `ʒ`, `ng` $\rightarrow$ `ŋ`, `kp`, `gb`, `ny`, `ch`, `sh`), before single letters.
- **Rule 2: Boundary Preservation**: In compounds or prefix junctions where `n` and `g` belong to separate morphemes (e.g. `n-gbaa` '1SG-catch'), maintain the hyphen or boundary marker to prevent false assimilation to `ŋ`.

## 4. Special Characters & Glyph Preservation in NLP/ASR

In LLMs and Speech Recognition systems, stripping or folding `ɛ, ɔ, ŋ, ɣ, ʒ` into basic Latin `e, o, n, g, z` is a **catastrophic integrity error** that collapses critical phonemic contrasts:

1. **Vowel Collapse Examples**:
   - *pɛ* `[pɛ]` (to borrow) $\neq$ *pe* `[pe]` (to gather / heap).
   - *sɔŋ* `[sɔŋ]` (grass mat) $\neq$ *soŋ* `[soŋ]` (to assist / help).
2. **Consonant Collapse Examples**:
   - *paɣa* `[paɣa]` (woman / wife) $\neq$ *paga* `*[paɡa]` (illicit form).
   - *ʒɛm* `[ʒɛm]` (to despise) $\neq$ *zem* `[zem]` (to be equal / level).
   - *ŋun* `[ŋun]` (who / whoever) $\neq$ *nun* `*[nun]` (illicit form).

All production tokenizers and text cleaners in the Dagbani AI suite must enforce **strict preservation** of the five BGL extended glyphs.
